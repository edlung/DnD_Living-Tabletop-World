# 0035. Images: OpenAI through OpenRouter in play, a curated set, purposes, PostgreSQL storage

- Status: Accepted (owner product decisions Q6 of 2026-10-05 and 2026-10-15; technical design by
  the session)
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Stage: 330
- Decision type: product (provider, spending ceiling, curated set) and technical.
- Owner approval: Q6 — OpenAI image models only, accessed through OpenRouter (2026-10-05); "Набор —
  подписка, в игре — OpenRouter до $5" (2026-10-15): the curated Ravenford set is generated through
  Codex CLI on the owner's ChatGPT subscription and imported as DM-approved; in-play generation uses
  OpenRouter with a $5 ceiling. All art is GPT-made and follows the owner's concepts (ADR 0034).
  Full design: `docs/STAGE_330_DESIGN.md`.

## Context

The image port (ADR 0022) had only a placeholder generator. Players saw neutral fallbacks. Identity
must hold across variants; spending must be bounded; images must reach players only after DM
approval; storage and backup must be decided for production.

## Decisions

1. **Generators behind the port.** `openrouter-openai` (in play: OpenAI image model through
   OpenRouter's chat completions with image output; fixed style prompt; derived variants attach the
   approved canonical reference; output validated and re-encoded to WebP ≤ 1.5 MB); `placeholder`
   (tests, offline); `curated` (seed import only).
2. **Ceiling.** A durable image ledger reserves a worst-case amount before any provider call and
   settles to the reported cost; the ceiling is set only by the operator CLI with an authorization
   source; no stored ceiling or zero means no call. Initial ceiling: $5.
3. **Curated set.** Generated once in the concepts' style, committed as WebP with a manifest
   (prompt, references, hashes), imported through real commands and approved as DM on a fresh
   install; never regenerated by an install.
4. **Purposes.** Assets carry `purpose`: `image` (chosen by ADR 0023 tags as before), `figure` (a
   character's tabletop figure) and `battle-map` (a place's top-down map under the encounter grid).
   Additive fields in `PlayerAssetView` and `EntityPresentation`; lineage, approval and visibility
   unchanged.
5. **Storage.** Image bytes stay in PostgreSQL (`ltw_asset_contents`, ≤ 2 MB each): one store,
   transactional with lineage, included in Stage 350's dumps. Trigger to move to object storage:
   a dump larger than 2 GB or taking longer than 5 minutes.
6. **Privacy.** A request is built from the entity's description; DM-only notes enter only when the
   DM types them into the request. No image reaches a player before approval; refusals, timeouts and
   provider errors leave the neutral fallback.

## Consequences

- Identity drift is measured per image (`reports/STAGE_330_IDENTITY.md`); drifted variants are
  regenerated or reported.
- The DM gets approval controls (approve, discard, generate again, replace) in the client.
- Image routes are Gate 06 surfaces (cost, visibility, prompt injection through descriptions).

## Amendments

- 2026-10-16 (Stage 330 part A, implementation details within the design):
  - **Failure code.** A generator that makes no provider call because no ceiling is stored, the
    ceiling is reached, the ledger is unavailable, the canonical reference is not stored or is no
    raster picture (a placeholder SVG), or the curated set lacks the picture fails the job with a
    new asset failure `UNAVAILABLE`; refusals, timeouts and provider errors keep `REFUSED`,
    `TIMEOUT` and `PROVIDER_ERROR` (the worker's own "entity gone" stays `PROVIDER_ERROR`).
  - **Versions** count per purpose too: a `wounded` figure is version 1 beside a `wounded` image.
  - **Reported cost.** Recorded as reported, capped, never dropped; a cost above the reservation
    is logged without the amount.
  - **Purpose in history.** An event payload carries `purpose` only when it is not `image`, so
    every asset event type kept its version; the lineage the revision before wrote
    (`fixtures/history/5da1b05-asset-lineage.json`) reads as images and re-decides unchanged.
  - **Ledger tables.** Settlements are a third append-only table (`ltw_image_settlements`, at most
    one per reservation), beside `ltw_image_budget` and `ltw_image_charges`.
  - **Reservations.** $0.50 per call of `openai/gpt-5.4-image-2` and $0.20 of
    `openai/gpt-5-image-mini` (`apps/server/src/images/models.ts`): conservative worst cases until
    the live smoke reports real costs; each call settles to the reported cost.
  - **References.** A new reference is sent with two curated style references (downscaled); a
    derived variant with its canonical reference only, so nothing competes with its identity.
  - **Processing.** Re-encoding lowers WebP quality from 82 to 72 and 62 only if a picture would
    not fit 1.5 MB; a figure's black background becomes transparent and a battle map is cropped to
    4:3, as the curated set's processing does.
  - **Seed.** Both Ravenford fixtures use the campaign ID `ravenford`, so `seed:ravenford` creates
    one campaign and imports the set into it; the browser-test server imports it into its listed
    campaigns (`apps/server/src/testing/ui-image-campaigns.ts`). The import approves only a job
    that produced the committed picture (provider `curated`, the manifest's hash) and retries a
    failed job under the next derived ID (at most three); the server's asset worker leaves the
    import's requests (`<campaignId>.curated.…`) alone.
- 2026-10-16 (Stage 330, the live smoke and orphaned reservations):
  - **Orphaned reservations.** A job that stops between its reservation and its settlement (a
    crash, a killed process; the restarted worker runs the job again under a new reservation)
    leaves the reservation open, counted whole. Only the operator closes one, with the owner's
    recorded authorization: `pnpm ai:operator image-reservations --older-than-minutes <n>` lists
    unsettled reservations (read-only); `image-reservation-settle --charge <id>` settles one as the
    outcome `orphaned`, at the whole reservation (treated as spent) unless `--charge-micros` gives
    the amount the provider's own records show. A reservation younger than the longest generation
    bound plus margin (15 minutes) is refused, so a running job's reported cost always wins.
    Migration 0012 adds the outcome and the authorization table; each requires the other.
  - **Live smoke.** `pnpm images:smoke --generator <the server's generator>` drives a running
    local server over HTTP as the development DM and player: Tomas Reed is authored (or found),
    the portrait request the authoring commit itself queues is followed (a second request would be
    a second paid call), the job, picture and ledger record are read back, the player sees nothing
    before the DM approves and the portrait after. It never causes a text call that could be paid:
    while a play session is open no AI request is sent and the DM writes the proposal the local
    interpreter makes of the sentence; without one, the server can only use its local provider,
    and the trace must show that.
- 2026-10-16 (Stage 330 part B, the client):
  - **DM controls.** The selection's panel (DM perspective only) and the DM's Images sheet read
    the lineage (`GET …/assets`) and send `RequestAsset`, `ApproveAsset` and `RetractAsset`
    through the command route; Discard is the retraction of a candidate. Replace asks for another
    picture of the same kind, and the current one stays for players until the DM approves the new
    one; Generate again and Try again repeat a request with its own note. A job shows its lineage
    status and its fixed failure reason, never progress. The note field says it reaches the image
    model; a variant, figure or battle map is offered only with an approved main picture.
  - **Live coverage.** The DM perspective's live coverage includes the lineage, so the controls
    follow the asset worker from the request to the candidate without polling; no lower
    perspective covers it (`apps/server/src/live/inventory.ts`).
  - **Figures and battle maps.** A figure stands upright on the tilted board, inside the medallion
    on the flat one and in the medallion's place in the scene; people keep their portraits in the
    panel, the HUD, the party rail and the conversation. The battle map replaces the UI kit's
    ground while the viewed place's presentation lists the encounter's combatants (the encounter
    view names no place). A thing's or a place's approved image is a plate in its panel.
  - **Test server.** The browser-test server runs the asset worker with the placeholder generator
    for the DM journeys' campaigns only (`apps/server/src/testing/ui-image-journeys.ts`); its
    review campaigns hold curated pictures as candidates not yet approved, so the evidence
    screenshots show GPT pictures only.
- 2026-10-16 (Stage 330 part B review: the design's rule for Replace, the client's honesty):
  - **An approval supersedes its kind.** Approving a candidate makes it the approved picture of
    its kind and supersedes the approved pictures of that kind — the same entity and purpose, and
    for an image the same label (the domain's `sameKind`; a figure's or a battle map's label
    chooses nothing, so any two figures of an entity are one kind). A reference still supersedes
    the canonical reference (`previous`); a derived picture lists what it superseded
    (`supersedes`). So the picture approved last is the one players see, even when it was made
    before the one it replaces, and the DM's list approves it alone. Undo restores every
    superseded picture; while a later approval of the kind stands, the earlier approval cannot be
    undone beneath it; a superseded picture keeps its request's undo hold, as a superseded
    reference always did; retracting the approved picture leaves its kind without one. A DM-only
    picture supersedes the players' one of its kind, as a DM-only reference does.
  - **`AssetApproved` version 3.** The decision changed, so the type has its next version (read
    alike). Version 2 approvals (Stages 150 to 330 part B) were decided when only a reference
    superseded anything, and an earlier writer could leave two pictures of a kind approved: they
    are canon as recorded — the re-decision oracle folds them instead of re-deciding them, and
    they rest on everything before them (ADR 0006 retest 3 amendment), so undo comes off such a
    history from its end backwards. The current writer's next approval of the kind supersedes
    all of them. Presentation is unchanged: of an earlier writer's several, the higher version (an
    image) or the one made last (a figure, a battle map) shows. The lineage revision 80d06df
    wrote (`fixtures/history/80d06df-approved-kinds.json`) loads, replays, re-decides and
    continues (`packages/combat/src/approval-history.test.ts`, `images.integration.test.ts`).
  - **What an approval says.** The client words an approval from the lineage read after it,
    never assumed: the main picture, a figure or a battle map is what players who can see the
    entity now see (a battle map under a fight there; approved state pictures keep their states);
    a state picture is "shown to players who can see <entity> <in its state>", with the caveat
    when another approved state picture may fit too; a DM-only picture, or one that is no longer
    the shown one of its kind, says so.
  - **One command at a time, visibly.** The controls come back only once a lineage read that
    started after the world's answer arrives, so the card the DM pressed is gone before anything
    can be pressed again (a second press would be a second, perhaps paid, request); while a read
    fails, the lineage is read again every 2 s. A command whose answer was lost holds every image
    control and shows its subject, its state and Retry in the Images sheet and in whichever
    entity's panel is open; Retry sends the same command ID, and its result shows where it was
    pressed. Controls that wait are `aria-disabled`, so focus stays on them.
  - **Focus.** When a command's result shows and focus went with the card it was pressed on,
    focus moves once — to the card that followed it, else the entity's heading in the sheet, else
    the panel's images or the sheet; a lost answer whose card went meanwhile moves focus to Retry.
  - **Name plates.** The battlefield's name plates lie in a layer of their own above every figure
    and cover piece, each where it lay in its token (which keeps its room) and scaled like the
    figure there, no wider than the room beside it in its row; tilted or flat, a figure in front
    never hides the plate, or the number, of a combatant behind it.
- 2026-10-16 (Stage 330 on the final clients of Stages 320 and 340, before its pull request):
  - **Plates and figures under the pointer.** Stage 320's hit-testing (design §5.5: what is drawn
    of a figure takes the pointer for its square) and the plates' layer meet: a name plate takes
    the pointer for its combatant's square — a press chooses it, the pointer lights it and the
    terrain card reads it — even where it lies over the figure of a combatant in front; a standing
    figure takes the pointer over its body like a portrait; the plate's room in the token stays
    inert. On the tilted board an occupied square's own plate lies over its centre, so browser
    tests point at a square where it shows.
  - **The Chronicle's pictures** (UI kit, ADR 0034 amendment): the candle-lit desk under the
    spread, the red ribbon as chapter tabs, the gold frame around the flashback's vignette.
