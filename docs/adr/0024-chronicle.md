# 0024. Chronicle: chapters from memories, traced prose, and flashbacks

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-04
- Stage: 170; reviewed at Astra Gate 05 with the integrated backend
- Decision type: product (owner-approved rendering, missing memories, persistence, and flashback dialogue) and technical
- Owner approval: on 2026-10-04, before implementation, the owner chose:
  1. deterministic entries are authoritative; AI prose is rendered from them only, cites them, keeps accounts and guesses marked, is traced, and changes nothing (§3);
  2. moments the party lived but does not remember are offered to the DM as suggested memories; the DM reviews and confirms them, and nothing is created automatically (§6);
  3. a chapter is generated by a DM command, `GenerateChronicle`, and recorded as `ChronicleGenerated` in the campaign history, as a record and never as a world fact (§4);
  4. a flashback's dialogue fragment quotes a related record the perspective may see, with its type; otherwise it shows the memory's summary (§7).

## Context

Inputs:

- `EXECUTION_PLAN.md` (Stage 170): select salient structured events and render Chronicle narrative with source event IDs; Chronicle prose is not canonical state.
- The work order adds:
  - non-DM Chronicle and flashbacks come from scoped memories (ADR 0010), not raw events;
  - epistemic types are preserved;
  - entries trace to their sources without exposing unauthorized source data;
  - Ravenford's minimal flashback: scene image, dialogue fragment, participants, world time.
- Validation gate: every material Chronicle claim traces to source events; prose cannot mutate state.
- `ADVERSARIAL_TESTS.md`:
  - A.5: Chronicle search for DM-only events;
  - C.2: an unverified hypothesis told as established history.
- `RAVENFORD_SPEC.md` names `GenerateChronicle` and `ChronicleGenerated`.

## Options considered

1. **Chronicle from raw events for everyone.** Rejected by ADR 0010: it would tell players events they never witnessed.
2. **AI prose as the chronicle.** The owner declined: prose cannot be checked claim by claim.
3. **Deterministic entries from memories (players) or salient events (DM), prose rendered from them and checked structurally, the chapter recorded.** Chosen.

## Decision

### 1. Entries for a perspective

- **Non-DM perspectives** are told from their own memories only (ADR 0010):
  - every memory the perspective may see whose world time lies in the window;
  - with its kind, place, participants, and salience;
  - with the related knowledge the perspective may see, each with its **epistemic type** (TESTIMONY, RUMOR, HYPOTHESIS, and so on) and statement.

  Another holder's memory, a DM record, or a raw event never enters such a chapter.

- **Selection.** At most 40 entries: the most salient, then shown in world-time order under opaque keys `e1…en`.

### 2. Entries for the DM

- The DM's chapter is told from **salient structured events**: effective events in the window, rated by a fixed table and told in fixed words with current names. Only events rated 3 or more are told. The rated events are:
  - deaths and destruction (5);
  - contradictions (5);
  - fights starting (4) and ending (3);
  - reactions used (4);
  - information shared (4);
  - examinations (3);
  - hypotheses (3);
  - a new day (3);
  - party members moving (3);
  - new NPCs (3).
- **Read by the DM alone.**

### 3. Prose (owner decision 1)

- **The renderer.** The `narrative` capability writes the prose from the entries alone: Anthropic then the local provider with a key, the local provider without one. The local writer is the deterministic template. The owner's fallback policy applies (ADR 0017 §5).
- **The prompt** holds the entries under their keys, with names, world times, and labelled accounts. It holds no IDs, and its data is JSON-quoted.
- **Rules**, checked by the renderer and again by the World Engine (`checkProse`):
  - every paragraph cites at least one key, and only keys of the chapter;
  - a paragraph citing an entry that rests on TESTIMONY, RUMOR, INFERENCE, HYPOTHESIS, CONTRADICTION, or UNKNOWN must say so in words, matched against a fixed hedge vocabulary such as "said", "suspected", "accounts", or "rumor";
  - prose holds no structured ID.
- **Fallback.** Anything else, after one repair, falls back to the **template**:
  - one paragraph per entry, each account labelled ("According to that account: …", "A suspicion, not established: …");
  - provider `template`, model `chronicle-template-1`.
- **Tracing.** Every rendering is traced (purpose `chronicle`). The trace maps each key to its source.

### 4. The chapter in history (owner decision 3)

- **Recording.** `GenerateChronicle { title, perspective, from, to, prose }` (DM or system; the generic command route refuses it) records `ChronicleGenerated`:
  - the chapter's ID, its title, its perspective, and its window;
  - the entries, recomputed by the World Engine itself;
  - the prose, checked against the entries;
  - `rests`, the facts the entries rest on.

  The server computes the entries, renders the prose, and submits the command with the version it computed against; a campaign that moved meanwhile is a conflict.

- **Refusals.** The decision refuses (`INVALID_CHRONICLE`):
  - an empty or future window;
  - a perspective the actor cannot take;
  - a chapter with nothing to tell;
  - prose that breaks §3.
- **What a chapter writes and reads.** A chapter is a record, never a world fact: no decision reads it, and its prose is never interpreted. It writes `chronicle:<id>`, and reads the clock and its `rests`:
  - each memory it tells;
  - the knowledge it cites;
  - for the DM's entries, the facts their events wrote.
- **Undo.** Undoing any of the facts a chapter rests on is refused while the chapter stands. The DM undoes the chapter first. A chapter can be undone.
- **Sources stay stable.** Entries name their sources by record or by command and event index, so replay and the re-decision oracle reproduce them exactly.

### 5. Reading chapters

- **Access.** `GET /campaigns/:campaignId/chronicle?perspective=…` lists the chapters told from that perspective; the DM reads all.
- **Sources.** Every entry's sources resolve to the effective events that recorded it: the memory's `MemoryRecorded` and its knowledge's `KnowledgeAssigned`, or the DM entry's own event.
  - Players see each source's event ID and type.
  - The DM also sees sequences and the rendering's trace ID.

### 6. Memory suggestions (owner decision 2)

- **The route.** `GET /campaigns/:campaignId/chronicle/suggestions?scope=PARTY|CHARACTER:<id>&from=…&to=…` (DM only) lists moments the scope's members lived through that no memory of the same kind within ten minutes covers:
  - a fight they were in (how many foes, how many fell, a countered spell);
  - a contradiction in their scope;
  - arriving at or returning to a top-level place;
  - a new day.
- **Drafts.** Each suggestion is a `RecordMemory` draft with its source events. Drafts name only what the scope knows; anything else is described.
- **Nothing is recorded** until the DM submits a draft, which the World Engine checks as any other memory (ADR 0010: presence, existence, citations).

### 7. Flashback (owner decision 4)

`GET /campaigns/:campaignId/memories/:memoryId/flashback?perspective=…` opens a memory the perspective may recall. It returns:

- the place as the perspective saw it at that world time, with its image (ADR 0023);
- the participants, with their presentations;
- the world time and day phase;
- a dialogue fragment: a related TESTIMONY, then RUMOR, the perspective may see, with its type and speaker; otherwise the memory's own summary;
- its sources.

Anything else is a 404, identical to a nonexistent memory.

## Consequences

- **The UI stages (200–230)** show chapters, their sources, suggestions, and flashbacks.
- **Gate 05** reviews this surface (`docs/GATE_COVERAGE.md`, row 170).
- **Hedging is checked lexically.** A provider could hedge in words while still implying a fact; the structured entries remain the authority, and the template never does.
- **Live quality.** The prose has not been run against the live API.

## Invariants affected

Implements:

- 2 and 7 (AI is no authority; its prose and suggestions never become facts or upgrade a hypothesis);
- 11 and 18 (auditable and traceable: every entry to its events, every rendering to its trace).

Preserves:

- 4 and 5 (player knowledge is not world truth; no leakage);
- 12 (reconstructable history);
- 3 (only the DM generates and records memories).

No invariant is weakened.
